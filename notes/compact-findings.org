#+title: Compact/Midnight upstream findings — for feedback to the team

were found (pointers at the bottom).

Every entry: what, evidence (checkable, not remembered), impact, and whether
anything deployed is affected.

RE-CHECKED 2026-08-28 (dmd: "many of them might be stale"), against the
first genuinely different compiler since our pin: compactc 0.34.0
(language 0.26.0) shipped 2026-08-25 as a FULL release. Method: the
0.34.0 binary fetched to a scratchpad, probe contracts compiled under
BOTH compilers, artifacts read with the new
=cargo run -p minocrab-ir --example zkir_text= disassembler (born for

One pin fact worth its own line: 0.34.0's flake.lock pins zkir-v3 at

* 1. WITHDRAWN 2026-08-28 — "a cast in argument position emits no range check" was a mis-read

THE ORIGINAL CLAIM (2026-08-16): =amount as Uint<64>= compiles to
nothing in argument position; the vault's four settle mints carry no
64-bit constraint; safety rests on a whole-contract map invariant.

First refuted 2026-08-19 (dmd session, against 0.33.0-rc.2, recorded
on the compact-bug-reports branch); re-confirmed and written up here
2026-08-28. REFUTED by probe and by re-reading the same artifacts:
- Probes (=scratchpad probes, argcast/chaincast/branchcast.compact=,
  compiled under 0.33.0-rc.2 AND 0.34.0): a =Uint<128> as Uint<64>= in
  argument position emits =constrain_bits 64= straight-line, and inside
  a conditional branch emits the GUARDED form —
  =cond_select(branch, ·, amount)= → =less_than(·, 2^64, bits=128)= →
  guarded =assert=. Both compilers, identical shapes.
- The corpus artifacts contain exactly that: claim has
  =ConstrainBits{amount, 64}= (instr 445), completeSwap four of them,
  and completeWithdraw/refund — whose mints sit on conditional branches
  — carry the guarded =LessThan(·, 2^64, bits:128)= + assert
  (completeWithdraw instr 477-480, refund instr 476-479).

WHY THE 2026-08-16 CHECK MISSED IT: it searched for a "64-bit op", and
the branched downcast runs its comparison at =bits: 128= against the
immediate =2^64= — the check was present, spelled in a shape the scan
did not recognize. The corrected understanding: compactc's downcast
check DOES cover argument position; in guarded contexts it lowers to
select/less_than/assert rather than =constrain_bits=.

CONSEQUENCES, corrected in the same sweep: the vault's settle mints are
LOCALLY sound (no whole-contract-invariant story needed);
notes/api-safety-survey.org §B4 and notes/builtin-lowering.org §9 carry
matching corrections; =BoundedUint::narrow='s rustdoc no longer claims
to emit "the check compactc omits" (it emits the same class of check
Compact's own downcast does — its value is making the checked/free
split explicit in OUR raw-wire API, which Compact's surface language
never exposes). What SURVIVES of the original concern: our API's
=from_field_unchecked= path (survey §A1) can still spell the unchecked
narrowing — but that is a fact about MinoCrab's escape hatch, not about
compactc, and it never belonged on this list.

* 2. GAP — kernel.checkpoint() cannot be compiled for ZKIR v3

STATUS 2026-08-28: STANDS at 0.34.0, probe-verified — the same
=failed assertion not-implemented= internal error (now at
reduce-to-zkir.ss:612 after the v3-pass restructuring; an
"unimplemented: ... ckpt" line names the construct in scheme-record
form, still no source location or flag named).

compactc's v2 backend assembles =ckpt= to 255 (=zkir-passes/
print-zkir.ss:546=); the v3 backend has no =ckpt= case, so
=--feature-zkir-v3= on a contract calling it dies with a bare =failed
assertion not-implemented=. Two deficiencies in one: the feature gap, and the
diagnostic (an assertion failure rather than an error naming the unsupported
construct and the flag that made it so).

Impact: any v3 contract using checkpoint. Evidence: notes/kernel-tokens.org
(M17 design note, failure quoted in the fixture header).

* 3. HAZARD — Boolean deserialization accepts non-canonical bytes

STATUS 2026-08-28: STANDS at 0.34.0, source-verified — the Boolean
arm of deserialize lowers to =(== byte 1)=
(analysis-passes/expand-serialize.ss:266-272 at the tag): any byte is
accepted and everything ≠ 1 is falsy, unchanged.

=deserialize<T, N>= does not constrain a deserialized Boolean to {0,1}: a
byte of 0x02 is accepted, and downstream =!b=-style routing then treats it as
falsy. In the erc20-vault this meant any attested byte ≠ 1 routed
completeWithdraw to the REFUND branch — a re-mint on a successful withdrawal
— found by the M10 property harness, and independently: a non-canonical
attested byte is ACCEPTED (both compactc's artifact and our mirror agree, so
it is the language's semantics, not a port bug).

Impact: any contract branching on a deserialized Boolean from
attacker-influenceable bytes. Our Borsh work closed it on our side (a Borsh
bool is 0|1 and nothing else); Compact's own deserialize still has it.
Evidence: notes/vault-optimization.org §"As built — step 1" (the harness
finding), notes/borsh-format.org §"Site classification" (the 0x02 hazard).

* 4. INEFFICIENCY — serialization lowering explodes bytes it could shift

STATUS 2026-09-05 (M31, the 0.34.0 bump): RE-MEASURED, STANDS UNMOVED.
The deferred bump-time bench was run: the Signet singleton (3 circuits)
and the vault's seventeen, recompiled from the same sources with both
0.33.0-rc.2 and 0.34.0 and priced with =minocrab_sim::v3::cost=. Every
one of the twenty artifacts is BYTE-IDENTICAL between the two
compilers, so no =k= crossed and no row moved — table in
notes/version-bump.org "Bump 1 — 2026-09-05" §"Step 6 — the numbers".
Whatever 0.34.0 improved in circuit generation, it did not touch the
explode/rebuild serialization chains this entry is about. The numbers
below stand as measured.

(Superseded, 2026-08-28: "NOT RE-MEASURED. 0.34.0's changelog notes
smaller circuits and better CSE, so the numbers may have moved; the
honest re-check is the bump-time bench.")

compactc lowers byte-reordering through full explode/rebuild chains (~150
rows per exploded byte) where ZKIR's own =ReverseBytes= (~150 rows TOTAL) or
algebraic div_mod shifts do the same work: measured, the mirrored chains cost
~4,600 rows where the native instruction costs ~150. On serialization-heavy
circuits the gap compounds: our segment-based re-limbing produces
byte-identical semantics at −97.5% rows on the Signet singleton's circuits
(respond: k15→k10, 8.24s→0.11s prove, 875MB→44MB). compactc also
over-serializes relative to its own model on refund (−12%) and completeSwap
(−15%) even before optimization.

Impact: prove time and RAM for every Compact user, no correctness issue.
Evidence: notes/benchmark.org ("M7 pass 1"/"pass 2" with per-circuit
numbers), BENCHMARK.md (the presentable three-way).

* 5. HAZARD — midnight-ledger main dropped instructions we (and they) ship

STATUS 2026-08-28: STANDS and has WIDENED — main's v3
=Instruction= enum now also lacks =Keccak256=, =IntoBytes32=/
=FromBytes32= and =Bytes32FromLowHigh=/=IntoLowHigh= (the whole
byte-plumbing family), gains a =Decode=, and stamps SerdeVersion 3.0.
Meanwhile the RELEASE line is at ledger-9.1.0.0-rc.4 (2026-08-11,
pre-release) and compactc 0.34.0 still pins zkir-v3 at =04c9c5d= (our
pin), so nothing shipping is affected yet — but the divergence between
main and every shipping artifact is now larger, not smaller.

A bump dry-run against midnight-ledger main (diverged from the release line;
merge base 2026-06-25) found it renamed midnight-ledger-v9, DROPPED
=Instruction::ReverseBytes= and the secp256r1/Curve25519 IrTypes. ReverseBytes
is emitted by shipping compactc releases (and spends ~4,500 rows of savings
per vault circuit in our lowering); if main ships as-is, existing v3
artifacts stop verifying against the new crates.

Impact: forward-compatibility of every deployed v3 circuit using those
instructions/types. Evidence: notes/version-bump.org (the recorded dry run,
with the worktree transcript).

* 6. RELEASE HYGIENE — identical version strings, different code

STATUS 2026-08-28: NOT RE-CHECKED for 0.34.0 (verifying

The ledger rc.3 tag's zkir-v3 crate PREDATES the binary bundled in the same

* 7. MINOR — constant naming is frontend-driven, not rule-driven

STATUS 2026-08-28: NOT RE-CHECKED in detail; 0.34.0's CSE/folding
changes make movement here LIKELY, which is fine — the normalisation
(=fold_immediate_copies= over both sides) is version-agnostic by
design.

compactc sometimes names a constant (=copy 0x00= then uses the name) and
sometimes inlines the same constant, with the choice driven by frontend
internals rather than any property of the ZKIR — =sSendShielded='s zero limb
is named and used both as a hash operand and through a =cond_select=, while
identical constants elsewhere are inlined. Harmless to semantics; costs
anyone doing instruction-level comparison a normalisation pass (ours:
=fold_immediate_copies= run over BOTH sides).

Evidence: notes/ir-passes.org §8 ("§1's table was WRONG, and the fixture
that proved it").

* 8. LATENT — the coin-arm `dup` reach overflows its nibble past depth 5

STATUS 2026-08-28: STANDS at 0.34.0 verbatim — the same "note that
if `f` is longer than 5, this exceeds the limit of 15" comment over the
same unchecked =dup= (midnight-ledger.ss:577 at the tag).

The `insertCoin`/`pushFrontCoin` lowering computes its `dup` reach from the
field path length, and the encoding has room for a nibble; a source comment at
=midnight-ledger.ss:576-577= notes the overflow rather than checking it, so a
coin collection nested deeper than 5 would miscompile silently. Unreachable
today (compactc's own kind-checking permits nesting only through =Map=, and no
corpus contract nests coin collections at all), so latent rather than live —
but it is a comment where a compile error should be.

Impact: none observed; a check would cost one line. Evidence:
notes/coin-arms-nested-adts.org (the 2026-08-16 investigation; the scheme
source is in corpus/src/compact).

* 9. MINOR — 0.34.0 lost the "try --feature-zkir-v3" diagnostic for secp256k1

FOUND 2026-09-05, at the M31 bump (0.33.0-rc.2 -> 0.34.0), by reading
the corpus compile-report diff: two sources changed their first-error
line without changing whether they compile.

compactc 0.33.0-rc.2, on a source using =Secp256k1Base= / =Secp256k1Scalar=
without =--feature-zkir-v3=:

: Exception: arithmetic.compact line 18 char 1:
:   secp256k1 is not supported in ZKIR v2: try recompiling with the flag `--feature-zkir-v3`

compactc 0.34.0, same source, same invocation:

: Exception: arithmetic.compact line 46 char 35:
:   unbound identifier Secp256k1Scalar

The cause is CHANGELOG 0.33.118: the point and scalar types stopped
being built-ins and became standard-library names injected from
=midnight-natives.ss= / =zkir-v3-natives.ss=. On the v2 path they are
now simply not injected, so the type name is unbound and the message
that named the fix is gone. A user on the default (v2) path who writes
=Secp256k1Scalar= is told the identifier does not exist, which invites
the wrong repair — spelling, imports — instead of the flag.

Evidence, checkable: =corpus/src/compact/examples/secp256k1/arithmetic.compact=
and =corpus/src/compact/compiler/zkir-v3-library.compact= under both
release binaries; the first of the two COMPILES cleanly under 0.34.0
with =--feature-zkir-v3=, so the flag really is the whole remedy.
Recorded in notes/version-bump.org "Bump 1 — 2026-09-05".

Impact: diagnostics only, no correctness issue, nothing deployed. The
fix would be to keep an "unbound because this needs --feature-zkir-v3"
hint for the injected names, or to inject them on the v2 path solely to
produce the old error.

* 10. SOUNDNESS GAP — =Bytes<32> as Uint<n>= / =as Field= accept byte strings ≥ p as their value mod p (2026-10-02)

What: compactc lowers a =Bytes<32>= to two limbs (248-bit low, 8-bit high) and the cast to =Field=/=Uint<n>= to =reconstitute_field(divisor = high, modulus = low, bits = 248)= followed by =constrain_bits n=. In-circuit (zkir-v3 =ir_vm.rs:1025=) =reconstitute_field= asserts =high < 2^7= and =low < 2^248= and then takes the linear combination mod p — nothing enforces the composite ≤ p−1; only the off-circuit VM (=ir_vm.rs:412=) rejects it. Byte strings in [p, 2^255) are therefore satisfiable witnesses whose cast result is the value mod p, so a prover running its own witness generator can present a 256-bit value ≥ 2^128 and pass =as Uint<128>= with a small result.

Evidence: =/tmp/u128-bench/direct.zkir= (compactc 0.34.0, =--feature-zkir-v3=), five instructions reproduced in notes/bytes32-to-u128.org; the two code paths quoted from the pinned zkir-v3 rev 04c9c5d. The zkir-semantics note already lists "ReconstituteField overflow" as an unenforced premise (I4 row, upstream §6.6(d)); this entry is the compactc-surface consequence: a Compact =as= cast that is exact for honest provers and not for adversarial ones.

Impact: any circuit that casts an adversarially-chosen =Bytes<32>= to =Field= or =Uint<n>= and relies on the result equalling the bytes' LE value. Workaround from Compact: assert the high byte zero first (=(x as Vector<32, Uint<8>>)[31] == 0=, 3 rows — the div_mods are DCE'd), which bounds the composite below 2^248 < p. Whether anything deployed is affected: not assessed (the vault's =as Field= sites on hash outputs would need a per-site look at where the bytes come from).

Documentation status (checked 2026-10-02): the Compact reference ("Casts of Bytes to and from Field and Uint", https://docs.midnight.network/compact/reference/compact-reference) promises "A dynamic error occurs if the result exceeds the maximum value of the Field or Uint type" — stated as the language semantics, with no distinction between the generated JS/witness path and the proving circuit. The zkir-v3 doc comment on =ReconstituteField= (ir.rs:684) claims it is "guaranteeing that the result does not overflow the field size". The ZKIR v3 spec (midnightntwrk/midnight-zkir PR #16, merged 2026-09-23, =zkir-spec/docs/zkir-v3-spec.md=) is the only place the gap is written down: §6.6(d) lists "ReconstituteField no-overflow" among the premises "the in-circuit lowering does not enforce", §7.2 tags "no field overflow, 2ⁿ·d+m < |Fr|" as obligation O4, and §8.4 states that O4 "rules out only adversarial witnesses" and that statement soundness holds "up to the slack O4 excludes". O4 is a condition on the witness, not on the source, so nothing obliges the Compact compiler to discharge it — the Compact-level "dynamic error" promise and the spec's carve-out are inconsistent, and nothing at the Compact level warns the contract author. That inconsistency is the reportable item.
